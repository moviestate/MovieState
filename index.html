Where to watch

Find the right stream in under a minute

  1. Open the film guide. Streaming, rental and purchase options are listed for the US, UK, Canada, Australia, India and Bangladesh.
  2. Check your region. Rights change per country, so a title can be included with one service and pay-per-view on another.
  3. Confirm quality on the provider. We link out; price, HD/4K and subtitles are set there.

How we work

Editorial standards, not scraped trivia

Data with a source

Titles, cast, crew, release dates and box office come from TMDB and are re-checked every time a page is rebuilt. When a figure is not reported, we say so.

Availability we verify

Watch options are grouped by country because rights are regional. Each guide names the services reported for that market and links to the provider to confirm price.

Written for decisions

Every guide answers three questions in order: what the film is, whether it fits your taste, and how to watch it. No autoplay, no pop-ups, no fake buttons.

FAQ

Questions people ask before pressing play

What is MovieState?

MovieState is an independent movie guide. Each page covers the story, cast, crew, runtime, ratings and release dates, plus where the film streams, rents or sells in major regions.

Is MovieState free to use?

Yes. Every guide on this site is free to read, with no account and no paywall.

Do you host or stream movies?

No. We publish information only and never host video files. We never host, embed or stream video files, and we do not link to unlicensed streaming services. Watch options point to official providers, or to a legitimate availability index such as JustWatch, where you can compare official services and current prices.

How do I find where to watch a movie?

Open a movie page and go to Where to watch. Availability is split into stream, rent and buy for the US, UK, Canada, Australia, India and Bangladesh. Confirm price and quality on the provider page.

Where does the movie data come from?

Posters, backdrops, cast, crew and ratings come from TMDB. Availability comes from TMDB provider data, so it can lag behind a service's own catalogue.

Why can availability differ from what I see?

Streaming rights are sold country by country and change over time. A film available in one region may not be licensed in another.

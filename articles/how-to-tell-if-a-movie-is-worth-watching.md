## 1. Read the vote count before the score

A 9.1 from 300 people means less than an 8.1 from 250,000. Small samples swing wildly on release week, and the first wave of votes is often the most enthusiastic audience. In our library, [12 Angry Men](/movie/12-angry-men-1957/) carries an 8.4 across decades of voting, while a brand-new release can show 8.5 on a few hundred votes and settle much lower a month later.

**Rule of thumb:** under 1,000 votes, treat the score as a rumour. Over 20,000, it is a signal.

## 2. Match the runtime to the evening you actually have

The most common reason people abandon a film halfway is not quality, it is timing. A 96-minute drama fits a weeknight; a 3-hour epic does not. Every MovieState guide lists the runtime on the page, so check it before you commit.

- **Under 100 minutes:** low risk, easy to finish
- **100 to 130 minutes:** standard feature, plan the evening
- **Over 150 minutes:** treat it as an event, not a filler

## 3. Look at the genre mix, not the genre label

A film tagged thriller and romance plays very differently from one tagged thriller and crime. Genre pairs tell you what the film is actually selling. If one of the two genres is not your thing, the film probably is not either.

## 4. Read the first paragraph of the synopsis, then stop

Synopses spoil more than they should after the second paragraph. One paragraph is enough to know the premise and the tone. If the premise does not interest you in three sentences, no rating will fix that.

## 5. Check whether you are walking into a franchise mid-story

Sequels inherit their audience. A 7.9 on the fifth entry means the fans were happy, not that a newcomer will be. Check the other films in the series first, and start at the beginning if the story is continuous.

## 6. Use the split between critics and audience as information

When professional reviews and audience scores disagree sharply, the film usually has a strong style or a strong fanbase. Neither side is wrong. Ask which side usually matches your taste, then weight that side.

## 7. The 60-second decision rule

Run the page in this order: rating and vote count, runtime, genre pair, one-paragraph premise, franchise check, then the official watch options. If two of the first four are a mismatch, skip it tonight and keep it on the list.

## 1. Start with one question: will you watch it more than twice?

That single answer decides most of the maths. A rental is priced for one sitting. A purchase is priced for many. A subscription is priced for volume, not for a specific film.

## 2. Subscription: best value only if you use it

A monthly subscription looks cheap per film only when you actually watch several films a month on that specific service. Most catalogues rotate, and the film you want is often on the one service you do not have.

**Practical approach:** keep one subscription active at a time. When the catalogue runs dry, switch, do not stack. Rotating one service every month usually costs less than holding three at once.

## 3. Rental: the default for a single evening

For a film you will watch once, a rental is almost always the cheapest legal option, and it comes with full quality and subtitles. Rentals are time-limited once started, so only press play when you are actually free.

## 4. Buying: only for rewatch value

Buying makes sense for films you return to, family titles that get replayed, and anything you want available offline. For a one-off evening it is the most expensive of the three.

| You will... | Usually cheapest |
|---|---|
| Watch once | Rent |
| Watch twice in a year | Rent twice or subscribe for a month |
| Rewatch regularly | Buy |
| Want a specific title tonight | Check availability first, then rent |

## 5. Check availability before you decide anything

Every [MovieState guide](/movies/) lists the official providers that carry the film, split by country, so you can see whether it is already included in a service you pay for. Availability changes without notice, so confirm the price and quality on the provider page before paying.

## 6. Three traps worth avoiding

- **Paying for a subscription to watch one film.** Rent it instead.
- **Renting a film you will rewatch with family.** Buy it once.
- **Trusting a “free full movie” page.** If a site offers a current release for free with no licence, the copy is unauthorised. It also tends to come with malware, card skimming and account bans.

## 7. A 3-step routine that works

1. Open the film guide and check which official services carry it in your country.
2. Decide once: rent, buy or subscribe, based on how many times you will watch.
3. Confirm the price on the provider page, then watch.

That routine takes two minutes and has saved more money than any deal alert.

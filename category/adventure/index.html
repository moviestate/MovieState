How to choose

What to look for in adventure films

Adventure movies sell a sense of scale — new worlds, long journeys, and stakes that grow with the map. They work best when the location feels like a character and the crew has a reason to keep moving.

Judge adventure films by how well they use their setting. A good one lets you remember routes and landmarks, and keeps the stakes personal even when the backdrop is huge.

Where to start: If you want family-friendly, start with the animated entries. If you want myth and scale, pick the longest film on the page and give it a full evening.

Frequently asked questions

What are the best adventure movies here?

Project Hail Mary, Avatar Aang: The Last Airbender and Swapped are the highest rated adventure guides here. Ratings are TMDB user scores and change over time.

Are adventure films suitable for children?

Many are, but ratings vary a lot. Animated adventures are usually PG, while live-action epics can carry PG-13 or R certificates. Check the certification on each film page before you watch together.

What is the difference between adventure and action?

Action is built around physical conflict. Adventure is built around travel and discovery, and the conflict is a consequence of the journey rather than the point of it.
